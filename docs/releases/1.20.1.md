# hyprmoncfg 1.20.1

Displays that can't wake straight into their saved mode now come back on. This release works with [panel 2.4.1](https://github.com/crmne/omarchy-hyprmoncfg/releases/tag/v2.4.1) for Omarchy; the panel doesn't need an update.

## A gentle wake for displays that drop right after connecting

Some displays can't wake straight into a demanding mode such as 4K at 144 Hz. The MSI MPG321UR-QD is one. When it's turned on, it drops off a second later, comes back showing "No signal", and goes back to sleep. That happens with VRR on or off. The step-down added in 1.20.0 never helped it, because it waited for three drops and this display only drops once before going quiet. Woken at 60 Hz and then switched to 144 Hz, it works every time.

Now one drop within 4 seconds of connecting is enough. The daemon remembers that the display needs a gentle wake:

1. It wakes the display at about 60 Hz, with the same resolution and VRR off.
2. Once the display has stayed connected for 10 seconds, it switches to your saved settings.

The first time, you see the drop once. After that, every power-on goes straight to the gentle wake. The daemon remembers this across restarts.

Displays that come up cleanly never drop, so they keep coming up in one step with no extra switch.

If the switch to your saved settings makes the display drop again, the daemon steps down as in 1.20.0: VRR off, then a lower refresh rate, then 60 Hz. It keeps the first one that sticks, and `hyprmoncfg status` says so. Applying a layout by hand tries the saved settings again, and keeps the gentle wake.

Your saved profile never changes. To make the daemon forget a gentle wake, delete `~/.config/hyprmoncfg/display-fallbacks.json` and restart it.

## Upgrading

Update both `hyprmoncfg` and `hyprmoncfgd`, then restart the service:

```sh
systemctl --user restart hyprmoncfgd.service
```

The IPC protocol is still version 1.

## What's tested

- **Automated tests** cover:
  - marking a display after one drop
  - writing the 60 Hz rule while the display is disconnected
  - switching to the saved settings after it settles, driven by the event loop
  - stepping down when that switch drops
  - remembering across restarts, and clearing on a manual apply
  - leaving displays that stay on alone
- **On the MSI MPG321UR-QD:** the 60 Hz, then 144 Hz sequence was done by hand, and it woke the display every time.
- **Not yet tried:** the daemon doing it by itself on a real display.
